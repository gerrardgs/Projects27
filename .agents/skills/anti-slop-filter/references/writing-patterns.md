# Writing Slop Patterns

For interface copy, marketing pages, documentation, release notes and commit messages written with AI. Look for **clusters**, not single tells: one em dash or one "however" proves nothing. Adapted from the [antislop copywriting skill](https://github.com/miqdadbadjuber/anti-slop/tree/main/skills/antislop-copywriting) (MIT, v3.2.21). Interface copy rules are in [14-ux-writing-and-ethics](../../../rules/14-ux-writing-and-ethics.md).

## Tone and voice

| Pattern | Example | Plainer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Empty vocabulary (unlock, elevate, empower, delve, seamless, robust, game-changer, journey, landscape, next-level) | "Unlock seamless collaboration to elevate your team's journey." | "Work with your team in one shared space." |
| Buzzwords (AI-powered, revolutionary, cutting-edge, next-generation, effortless, ultimate) | "Revolutionary AI-powered analytics." | Say what it does: "Flags failed payments within a minute." |
| Significance inflation | "A pivotal moment ushering in a new era of work." | "Cuts the time your team spends in status meetings." |
| Unsupported social proof | "Trusted by thousands of teams worldwide." | Name real customers, or cut the claim |
| Weasel attribution | "Experts say this improves conversion." | Name the source and the figure, or cut it |
| Authority tropes | "At its core, what really matters is..." | State the point directly |
| Chatbot closers | "I hope this helps! Let me know if you have questions." | End on the information |
| Fake-candid openers | "Honestly? Here's the thing." | Say the thing |
| Signposting | "Let's dive in. Here's what you need to know." | Start with the content |
| Capitals for emphasis | "WE MUST ACT NOW." | Write the urgency into the sentence |
| Actorless passive | "The pricing page was updated." | "We rewrote the pricing page." (passive is right when the actor is unknown or irrelevant) |
| Objects with human verbs | "The dashboard understands what you need." | "The dashboard opens on the three metrics you check daily." |

## Rhythm and structure

| Pattern | Sign |
| :--- | :--- |
| Rule of three | Every idea forced into a trio ("innovation, inspiration and insight") |
| Negative parallelism | "It's not just X, it's Y"; "no guessing, no waste" tacked on |
| Aphorism formulas | "X is the currency of Y" |
| Staccato drama | A run of fragments for a punchline ("No preference. No prior. No nostalgia.") |
| Synonym cycling | Renaming the same thing each sentence to avoid repetition; repeat the clearest word instead |
| False ranges | "From onboarding to scale, and everything in between" |

## Honesty and evidence

- **Fabricated specifics**: invented numbers, names, dates, quotes or features that look real. This is a defect even when it sounds human ([09-design-principles](../../../rules/09-design-principles.md) section 7).
- **Speculative gap-filling**: a guess written as fact ("likely founded in the 1990s"). Find the source or leave it out.
- **Generic upbeat ending**: "The future looks bright." End on the last real piece of information.

## Typography and formatting

- **Em dashes**: a frequent AI tell in agent-written copy. Default: use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets instead. A brand voice or writing sample that uses em dashes is direction: ask the owner, then follow the answer. This repository's own documentation avoids them.
- **Bold everywhere**: emphasis on every key term is emphasis on none.
- **Scare quotes**: quotation marks around ordinary words for distance or emphasis.
- **Inline-header lists and emoji in headings**: "🚀 **Speed:** ..." on every bullet.
- **Filler and hedging**: "in order to", "it is important to note that", stacked "may potentially".

## What not to flag

Polished grammar, formal words used precisely, a single transition word, curly quotes, one short emphatic sentence, unsourced everyday claims, and text inside quotations, titles, names or examples. Rewrite only where tells cluster, and keep the author's meaning.

## Signs of a human writer (keep them)

Specific, hard-to-invent details; mixed feelings; era-bound references; varied sentence length; genuine asides and self-corrections. When the user gives a writing sample, match its sentence length, vocabulary and punctuation instead of scrubbing it toward a neutral voice.
